"Just do pull-ups" is unhelpful advice if you can't do one yet — that's
the whole problem. The good news is the path to a first pull-up is well
established, and it doesn't require a lat pulldown machine you don't
have.

## Why pull-ups are harder than they look

A pull-up asks your back and arms to move your entire bodyweight through
a full range of motion, with no assistance. Most people — regardless of
general fitness — haven't trained that specific strength curve before.
It's not a sign anything's wrong; it's a sign the movement needs to be
built up in pieces.

## The four-step progression

**1. Dead hangs.** Just hanging from the bar, working up to 20–30
seconds, builds grip and shoulder stability most people skip entirely.

**2. Scapular pulls.** From a hang, pull your shoulder blades down and
back without bending your elbows much. This teaches the "engage before
you pull" pattern that makes the full movement possible later.

**3. Negative pull-ups.** Start at the top (jump or use a step) and
lower yourself as slowly as you can — aim for 3–5 seconds on the way
down. This is the single highest-leverage step in the whole progression.

**4. Banded or assisted pull-ups.** A resistance band looped over the
bar removes just enough weight to let you do full reps with good form.
Reduce the assistance as you get stronger.

## How long this actually takes

For most people starting from zero, 6–12 weeks of consistent training
(2–3 times a week) is a realistic timeline to a first unassisted
pull-up. That range is wide because it depends on your starting
strength-to-bodyweight ratio, not effort — someone lighter and already
doing some upper-body training will move through it faster than someone
starting from true zero.

## The mistake that stalls progress

Skipping straight to assisted pull-ups without building the hang and
negative first often stalls around the same sticking point for months.
The negative specifically trains control through the hardest part of the
range — skipping it means the assisted reps aren't actually preparing you
for the unassisted version.

## Where to start this week

If you can't hang for 20 seconds yet, start there. If you can, move to
negatives — three sets of three to five slow negatives, twice a week, is
enough to start seeing real progress within a month.
