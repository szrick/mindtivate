Protein advice tends to arrive as a slogan — "eat more protein" — without
a number attached. That's not useless, but it's not actionable either.
Here's what the research actually points to.

## The range that shows up across studies

For active women doing regular resistance training, research generally
supports somewhere around **1.6–2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body
weight per day**. For a 65kg (about 143lb) woman, that's roughly
104–143g daily. That's meaningfully higher than the general population
guideline (0.8g/kg), which was set to prevent deficiency, not to support
training adaptations.

If you're not doing structured resistance training, the lower end of
that range — or even below it — is reasonable. The higher end matters
most when you're actively building or preserving muscle.

## Why the number is higher during a calorie deficit

If you're eating in a deficit (intentionally or not), protein needs go
up, not down. Adequate protein during weight loss helps preserve lean
mass, so more of what you lose is fat rather than muscle. This is one of
the more consistent findings in the research — it shows up across
multiple studies on women specifically, not just general population
data.

## What this actually looks like in a day

Numbers are easier to act on when they're broken into meals. Roughly
25–35g of protein per meal, three to four times a day, gets most people
into range without needing to obsessively track. As a rough guide:

- A palm-sized portion of chicken, fish, tofu, or eggs is typically
  20–30g.
- Greek yogurt (150g) is around 15g.
- A protein shake is usually 20–25g.

## Where this can go wrong

The most common mistake isn't eating too little protein — it's treating
protein targets as a reason to under-eat everything else. Protein
supports training; it doesn't replace the need for enough total energy,
carbohydrates for performance, or fat for hormone health. A target is a
floor, not the whole plan.

## The takeaway

If you're training regularly, aim toward the 1.6–2.2g/kg range and split
it across three to four meals. You don't need to hit it exactly every
day — consistency over a week matters more than any single day's total.
