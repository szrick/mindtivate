"I'm sorry you feel that way" and "I'm sorry I did that" repair trust
very differently, and research on effective apologies backs up why the
difference matters.

## The components that matter most

Acknowledging the specific action (not just the other person's feelings),
and a credible plan to avoid repeating it, are consistently rated as the
most important components of an apology that actually rebuilds trust.

Apologies that skip straight to "let's move on" without either of those
tend to feel unresolved, even when the words technically said sorry.
