Advice about *when* to eat — not skipping breakfast, not eating late,
spacing meals a certain way — is common, but the evidence for timing
mattering as much as total daily intake is weaker than the advice implies.

## What the research actually supports

For most people, total daily intake and food quality explain far more of
the outcome than the specific clock time food is eaten. Timing can matter
at the margins — for athletes optimizing performance, or for people whose
schedule makes late, large meals disrupt sleep — but it's a secondary lever,
not the primary one.
