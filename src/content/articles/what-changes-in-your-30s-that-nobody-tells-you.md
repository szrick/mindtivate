"Your metabolism slows down" is the thing everyone says about turning
30, and it's true, but it's also the least useful part of the story.
The bigger shifts are in recovery, muscle, and bone — and they matter
more for how you'll actually feel in your 40s and 50s than a slightly
lower calorie burn does today.

## Recovery takes longer, and that's normal

Workouts that used to leave you fine the next day can start requiring
real recovery time — more sleep, more protein, more spacing between
hard sessions. This isn't a sign anything is wrong; it's a normal
shift in how the body repairs itself. Training the same way you did
at 22 without adjusting recovery is a common way people end up injured
or burned out, then wrongly conclude they "can't do this anymore."

## Muscle mass starts to matter more than the scale

Muscle mass naturally begins a slow decline starting in your 30s if
it isn't actively maintained — a process that accelerates with age.
Strength training becomes less about aesthetics and more about
protecting long-term mobility, metabolism, and bone density. This is
also the decade where building a resistance training habit pays off
disproportionately later, since it's easier to maintain muscle you
already have than to rebuild it after losing it.

## Bone density peaks and then declines

Peak bone mass is typically reached by your late 20s to early 30s,
after which it gradually declines — which is exactly why weight-
bearing exercise in your 30s isn't just about how you look now, but
about the density you're carrying into perimenopause and beyond, when
bone loss accelerates further.

## What to actually do differently

Prioritize protein and resistance training more deliberately than you
needed to in your 20s, build in real recovery instead of pushing
through fatigue, and treat consistent sleep as part of your fitness
routine, not separate from it. None of this is about slowing down —
it's about training for the decade you're actually in.
