Peak bone density is typically reached by the late 20s to early 30s, well
before most people start thinking about bone health — which changes the
strategy at different life stages.

## What matters at each stage

In your 20s and early 30s, weight-bearing exercise and adequate calcium and
vitamin D intake help build density that acts as a buffer later. After that,
the priority shifts toward maintaining density and mitigating the more
noticeable loss that tends to accelerate around perimenopause and beyond.

Starting strength training earlier doesn't just build muscle — it's one of
the more effective ways to influence a factor (bone density) that gets much
harder to build back later.
