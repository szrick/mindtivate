Skincare marketing has made "routine" mean "many steps." Most of what
actually changes your skin over time comes from a short list of
habits, done consistently, not a shelf of products used once each.
Everything past that short list is optional — sometimes genuinely
nice, but optional.

## The non-negotiables

Sunscreen, daily, is the single highest-leverage thing you can do for
how your skin looks in ten years — most visible aging is sun exposure,
not time. A gentle cleanser that doesn't strip your skin, and a
moisturizer that matches your skin's actual needs, round out the base.
That's three products. Everything else is layered on top of that
foundation, not a replacement for it.

## What's worth adding, if you want more

Retinoids have some of the best evidence behind them for texture and
fine lines, but they need to be introduced slowly — start a couple of
nights a week, not every night, to avoid irritation. Vitamin C serums
have reasonable evidence for brightening and antioxidant support. Both
are genuinely useful additions, not essential ones.

## What you can skip without guilt

Multi-step routines built around trend products — the ingredient of
the month, a new "must-have" serum every season — mostly add cost, not
results. Your skin doesn't need eight active ingredients layered at
once; several actives on top of each other more often causes
irritation than extra benefit. If a step doesn't have a clear job,
it's fine to leave it out.

## The actual formula

Protect it, clean it gently, keep it moisturized, and add one or two
proven actives if you want to do more. That's the whole routine that
matters. Everything else on a skincare shelf is a preference, not a
requirement.
