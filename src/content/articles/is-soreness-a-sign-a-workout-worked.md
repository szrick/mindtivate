It's tempting to use next-day soreness as a scoreboard — sore means it
worked, no soreness means it didn't. Research on delayed-onset muscle
soreness (DOMS) doesn't support that as a reliable signal.

## Soreness tracks novelty, not effectiveness

DOMS is most closely tied to unfamiliar movement patterns and eccentric
loading, not to how much muscle growth or strength adaptation actually
happened. A well-trained lifter can make consistent progress with
comparatively little soreness once their body adapts to a routine.

That means chasing soreness as a goal can backfire — it often means
under-recovering between sessions rather than training more effectively.
