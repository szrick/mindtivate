## Why deep forehead lines show up earlier than you'd expect

You're 30, and the lines across your forehead aren't fine—they're deep, visible even when your face is at rest, and they're making you self-conscious. It feels too early for this, but forehead wrinkles are one of the first signs of aging many women notice, especially if you're expressive (raising your eyebrows a lot) or spent years without consistent sun protection.

Those lines form because the skin loses collagen and elasticity over time, and repeated muscle movement—every time you lift your brows—creases the skin in the same spots. Eventually, those creases don't bounce back. Sun exposure accelerates the breakdown of collagen, which is why even younger skin can develop deeper lines if it's been unprotected.

Facials feel like they should help, but the truth is they won't do much for established wrinkles. A facial can temporarily plump the skin with hydration or improve texture, but it doesn't change the underlying collagen loss or the depth of a wrinkle that's already set in. For that, you need ingredients that actually affect how skin cells behave.

## What topical treatments have real evidence behind them

The ingredient with the strongest research for reducing wrinkles is **retinol**—or its prescription-strength relatives, retinoids like tretinoin. [Retinoids work by increasing cell turnover and stimulating collagen production](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/anti-aging/retinoid-retinol), which over time can soften the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles. They won't erase a deep line completely, but they can make it less pronounced.

Retinol is available over the counter in serums and creams; tretinoin typically requires a prescription (though regulations vary by country). If you've never used a retinoid before, start with an over-the-counter retinol a few nights a week. Your skin will likely flake, turn red, or feel tight as it adjusts—this is normal and usually settles down after a few weeks. Apply it at night (retinoids break down in sunlight), and use a pea-sized amount for your whole face.

**Vitamin C** is the next-best-supported ingredient for wrinkles. Multiple controlled trials have shown that topical vitamin C at 10-15% concentrations can produce statistically significant improvements in fine lines over 12 weeks to 6 months. It works by supporting collagen synthesis and protecting against sun damage—and unlike retinol, you can use it in the morning.

Other ingredients that show up in the research include **peptides** (short chains of amino acids that may signal skin to produce more collagen, though human studies are more limited) and **niacinamide** (which can improve skin texture and barrier function). These won't work as dramatically as retinoids, but they can support overall skin health and may enhance the effects of retinol when used together.

## The one non-negotiable that protects what you're building

No anti-aging routine works without sunscreen. If you're using retinol to build collagen but going outside without SPF, you're undoing your progress. UV exposure breaks down collagen faster than retinol can rebuild it.

Use a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every single day, even when it's cloudy, even in winter. This isn't about preventing new wrinkles—it's about protecting the skin repair you're working toward. Sunscreen is the difference between retinol that works and retinol that spins its wheels.

## How long it actually takes (and what to expect)

Retinol doesn't work overnight. Most people start to see a difference in fine lines around 12 weeks of consistent use; deeper wrinkles take longer—sometimes six months or more—and even then, the improvement is usually a softening, not a disappearance. But don't treat six months as the endpoint: clinical studies show that improvements can continue up to 12-24 months with consistent tretinoin use, so patience pays off.

That's not a failure of the product. Deep wrinkles represent years of collagen loss and repeated muscle movement. A topical treatment can improve the skin's structure and make the line less obvious, but it can't fully reverse what's already there. If you're looking for more dramatic results, in-office treatments like [Botox (which relaxes the muscle causing the crease)](/articles/under-eye-botox-made-my-creases-worse-what-happened/) or laser resurfacing (which stimulates deeper collagen remodeling) are worth discussing with a dermatologist.

But for a 30-year-old just starting to address forehead lines? A [consistent routine with retinol and sunscreen](/articles/how-to-restart-a-skincare-routine-after-years-of-nothing/) is the foundation. It's not glamorous, it's not fast, and it won't give you before-and-after photos in two weeks—but it's what actually works over time, and it's a whole lot more effective than a facial.
