A warm-up isn't just about breaking a sweat — it's about raising tissue
temperature, priming the specific joints and muscles you're about to load, and
rehearsing the movement pattern at low intensity before you add weight to it.

A general 5-minute cardio warm-up covers the first goal but skips the other
two, which is why a lift can still feel stiff or uncoordinated even after
"warming up."

## What an effective warm-up actually includes

- **2–3 minutes of light cardio** to raise heart rate and core temperature.
- **Dynamic mobility** for the joints you're about to load — hips and
  shoulders for most lower/upper body lifts.
- **Ramp-up sets** of the actual exercise, working up from an empty bar or
  light weight to your working load in 2–3 jumps.

Skipping the ramp-up sets is the most common shortcut, and it's the one that
matters most for how the first working set feels.
