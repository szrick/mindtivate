Mood changes on hormonal birth control are a common enough complaint
that research has looked specifically at why some people notice them and
others don't.

## What tends to predict a stronger effect

A personal or family history of depression, and starting birth control
during adolescence, are both associated with a higher likelihood of
mood-related side effects in research on the topic.

That doesn't mean birth control isn't right for someone with that history —
it means it's worth tracking mood deliberately for the first few months and
raising it with a doctor if it doesn't settle.
